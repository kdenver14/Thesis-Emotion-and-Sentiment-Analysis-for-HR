MELD RELIABILITY DEFENSE — "Why Use a TV Show Dataset for HR?"
================================================================

THE DIRECT ANSWER
-----------------
MELD isn't a perfect match for HR — no publicly available dataset is.
But it's the best available option, and here's why it still works:


1. EMOTIONS ARE UNIVERSAL, CONTEXT IS NOT
------------------------------------------
The 7 emotions the model classifies (anger, joy, sadness, fear, disgust,
surprise, neutral) are based on Ekman's basic emotions — these are universal
across humans regardless of setting. A person showing anger in a TV show and
a person showing anger in an interview activate the same facial muscles,
vocal patterns, and word choices.

What changes is WHEN and WHY they appear — that's what HR interprets, not
the model. The model just detects WHAT emotion is present.


2. WHY NOT USE REAL HR INTERVIEW DATA?
---------------------------------------
- Privacy and ethics — recording real job interviews and labeling people's
  emotions for a public dataset would require massive consent frameworks,
  IRB approval, and legal review. No such dataset exists publicly.

- IEMOCAP — lab-recorded, only 2 speakers, ~12 hours. Too small, too
  controlled.

- CMU-MOSEI — YouTube monologues, no conversation. Interviews are
  conversations.

- RAVDESS — actors reading scripted sentences. Even more artificial than
  MELD.

MELD has 13,000+ utterances, multiple speakers per conversation, natural
turn-taking, and all three modalities aligned. No other public dataset
checks all those boxes.


3. WHY MELD CONVERSATIONS ARE CLOSER TO INTERVIEWS THAN YOU'D THINK
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- Multi-party dialogue (like a panel interview)
- Speakers react to each other in real-time (like Q&A)
- Emotional shifts happen mid-conversation (like when a tough question lands)
- Speakers sometimes mask or suppress emotions (like candidates do)

The actors in Friends aren't reading from a lab script — they're performing
naturalistic dialogue with realistic emotional dynamics.


4. THE HONEST LIMITATION (say this — it shows maturity)
--------------------------------------------------------
"We acknowledge that a model trained on conversational TV data may not
perfectly capture the subtleties of professional settings — for example,
candidates in interviews tend to display more subdued emotions and more
masking behavior than TV characters. Ideally, a fine-tuning step on a
labeled HR interview dataset would improve domain accuracy. However, given
the ethical and legal constraints of collecting such data, MELD provided the
strongest available foundation for a multimodal system. Our engagement
scoring and emotional trajectory features are designed to work on top of
relative emotional patterns rather than absolute classifications, which
makes them more robust to this domain gap."


5. IF THEY PUSH FURTHER — "How do you know it works on real interviews?"
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Be honest: we validated on MELD's test split, not on real HR recordings.
That's a limitation. But:

- The system outputs CONFIDENCE SCORES, not binary labels — HR sees
  "72% neutral, 15% joy, 8% fear" not just "happy"

- The engagement score uses RATIOS AND PATTERNS (positive vs negative,
  variety, trajectory), which are relative — they work even if absolute
  emotion accuracy shifts across domains

- This is a PROOF OF CONCEPT for BlastAsia. A production system would
  include a fine-tuning phase on their actual (consented) recordings

That's the strongest position: honest about the gap, clear about why it's
the best available choice, and specific about what a production path
looks like.


6. HOW HR ACTUALLY BENEFITS DESPITE THE DOMAIN GAP
----------------------------------------------------
The system doesn't replace HR judgment — it augments it with data.

Without the system:
  HR watches a 1-hour interview, writes subjective notes, forgets half of it.

With the system:
  HR gets per-speaker emotional breakdown, stress peaks flagged with
  timestamps, engagement score, and a summary — then applies their human
  judgment on top of concrete data.

7. ETHICS QUESTION — "Is this ethical?"
----------------------------------------
If a panelist asks this:

1. The system is for RECORDED interactions (not surveillance or real-time
   monitoring)
2. All parties should CONSENT to being recorded and analyzed
3. The system provides SUPPLEMENTARY DATA, not automated decisions — no one
   gets hired or fired by the AI
4. Results are ISOLATED PER USER (userId scoping in database) — one HR
   manager can't see another's data
