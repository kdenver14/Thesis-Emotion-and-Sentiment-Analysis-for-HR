MELD DATASET — THESIS DEFENSE DEEP DIVE
=========================================


================================================================================
WHERE DOES MELD COME FROM?
================================================================================

MELD stands for "Multimodal EmotionLines Dataset." It has a two-step origin:

STEP 1 — EmotionLines (2018)
  Authors: Sheng-Yeh Chen, Chao-Chun Hsu, Chuan-Chun Kuo, and others
  Published: LREC 2018
  What it was: A TEXT-ONLY emotion dataset of multi-party conversations
  extracted from the TV series "Friends." It contained dialogue
  transcripts with emotion labels but NO audio or video data.

STEP 2 — MELD (2019)
  Authors: Soujanya Poria, Devamanyu Hazarika, Navonil Majumder,
           Gautam Naik, Erik Cambria, and Rada Mihalcea
  Published: ACL 2019 (Association for Computational Linguistics) —
             one of the top-tier NLP conferences
  What they did: Extended EmotionLines by adding the AUDIO and VIDEO
  modalities. They retrieved the original Friends TV episodes,
  aligned each utterance from EmotionLines to its corresponding
  video clip, and extracted the audio and visual data. The same
  dialogue instances and emotion labels were preserved, but now
  each utterance has text + audio + video.

  Paper: "MELD: A Multimodal Multi-Party Dataset for Emotion
         Recognition in Conversations"
  ArXiv: arxiv.org/abs/1810.02508
  Website: affective-meld.github.io
  Repository: github.com/declare-lab/MELD

So MELD is the multimodal upgrade of EmotionLines — same conversations,
same labels, but now with all three modalities that our system needs.


================================================================================
WHAT DOES MELD CONTAIN? (EXACT NUMBERS)
================================================================================

  Total dialogues:    1,433
  Total utterances:   13,708
  Source:             Friends TV series (seasons 1-9)

  SPLIT         DIALOGUES    UTTERANCES
  Training        1,098        9,989
  Development       114        1,109
  Test              280        2,610

  EMOTION LABELS (7 classes):
    Anger, Disgust, Fear, Joy, Neutral, Sadness, Surprise

  SENTIMENT LABELS (3 classes):
    Positive, Negative, Neutral


  MODALITIES per utterance:
    - Text:  Dialogue transcript (the spoken words)
    - Audio: Raw audio clip from the TV episode
    - Video: Raw video clip from the TV episode

  SPEAKERS: Multi-party (2-7+ speakers per dialogue, typically the
  6 main Friends characters plus guest actors)


================================================================================
WHY DID WE USE MELD? (THE CORE ARGUMENT)
================================================================================

We needed a dataset that satisfies ALL of these requirements:
  1. Has TEXT + AUDIO + VIDEO for every utterance (multimodal)
  2. Has per-utterance emotion labels (not just per-video)
  3. Has multiple speakers per conversation (multi-party)
  4. Is large enough to train a deep learning model (10,000+ samples)
  5. Is publicly available and freely accessible
  6. Has standardized train/dev/test splits for reproducible benchmarks

Here's why MELD was the only real choice:

DATASET         TEXT  AUDIO  VIDEO  MULTI-PARTY  SIZE       PUBLIC
-----------     ----  -----  -----  -----------  --------   ------
MELD            YES   YES    YES    YES (2-7+)   13,708     YES
IEMOCAP         YES   YES    YES    NO (dyadic)   5,531     Restricted
RAVDESS         NO    YES    YES    NO (single)   7,356     YES
CMU-MOSEI       YES   YES    YES    NO (single)  23,453     YES
EmotionLines    YES   NO     NO     YES           12,894    YES
SEMAINE         YES   YES    YES    NO (dyadic)   ~5,000    Restricted

KEY REASONS:

1. ONLY LARGE-SCALE MULTIMODAL MULTI-PARTY DATASET
   IEMOCAP and SEMAINE are dyadic (exactly 2 speakers). In real HR
   interviews, you often have 2-3+ participants (interviewer, candidate,
   panel members). MELD is the only large dataset with more than 2
   speakers per dialogue, which made it uniquely suited for our
   multi-speaker HR use case.

2. ALL THREE MODALITIES
   EmotionLines (MELD's predecessor) was text-only. RAVDESS has audio
   and video but no text transcripts. CMU-MOSEI has all three but is
   monologue (YouTube opinion videos), not conversational. MELD is the
   only dataset with all three modalities IN A CONVERSATIONAL SETTING.

3. DUAL ANNOTATION (EMOTION + SENTIMENT)
   MELD provides BOTH emotion labels (7 classes) and sentiment labels
   (3 classes) for every utterance. This is why our model has two
   classification heads — we get both granular emotion detection AND
   broader sentiment analysis from a single forward pass. Most other
   datasets only have one or the other.


4. PUBLIC AND STANDARDIZED
   MELD is freely available with standardized splits. IEMOCAP requires
   institutional access agreements. This matters for thesis
   reproducibility — anyone can download MELD and replicate our results.

5. ESTABLISHED BENCHMARK
   Published at ACL 2019 (top-tier venue), MELD has become the standard
   benchmark for multimodal emotion recognition in conversations.
   Hundreds of papers report results on MELD, giving us baselines to
   compare against and validating that the research community considers
   it a reliable dataset.


================================================================================
WHY IS MELD RELIABLE?
================================================================================

1. PEER-REVIEWED PUBLICATION
   MELD was published at ACL 2019, one of the most prestigious NLP
   conferences. The paper underwent rigorous peer review. It has been
   cited 1,400+ times as of 2026, indicating widespread adoption and
   scrutiny by the research community.

2. MULTIPLE ANNOTATOR AGREEMENT
   The emotion labels in MELD were annotated through a structured
   process. The original EmotionLines paper used multiple annotators
   per utterance and measured inter-annotator agreement. Labels were
   assigned based on majority vote, reducing individual annotator bias.

3. NATURAL CONVERSATIONAL CONTEXT
   Unlike acted datasets (RAVDESS, where actors deliberately perform
   emotions in isolation), MELD's emotions arise in the context of
   ongoing conversations. Even though Friends is scripted, the actors
   deliver emotions in response to dialogue context — closer to how
   emotions emerge in real conversations. Each utterance exists within
   a dialogue flow, not as an isolated clip.

4. MULTI-PARTY DYNAMICS
   Real conversations involve interruptions, reactions, and emotional
   contagion between multiple people. MELD captures this. Datasets
   like IEMOCAP with only 2 actors in a controlled lab cannot.

5. BALANCED REPRESENTATION OF EMOTIONS
   While MELD is imbalanced (47% neutral), it contains meaningful
   samples of ALL 7 emotion classes including rare ones like disgust
   and fear. This is realistic — in real conversations, most utterances
   ARE neutral. The imbalance reflects natural emotional distribution,
   which is actually a strength for real-world application.

6. BROAD EMOTIONAL RANGE
   The Friends source material covers a wide range of emotional
   scenarios: arguments, celebrations, breakups, job interviews,
   awkward situations, surprises. This diversity means the model sees
   emotions in varied conversational contexts, not just one narrow
   domain.

7. COMMUNITY VALIDATION
   State-of-the-art models regularly benchmark on MELD. Recent results:
   - M2FNet (CVPR 2022): transformer-based fusion, 66.7% weighted F1
   - Various models achieve 60-67% weighted F1 on emotion classification
   - The fact that the research community consistently uses MELD as a
     benchmark validates its reliability and relevance

8. REPRODUCIBILITY
   Standardized train/dev/test splits mean every researcher evaluates
   on the exact same data. Results are directly comparable across papers.


================================================================================
MELD LIMITATIONS (BE HONEST ABOUT THESE)
================================================================================

1. DOMAIN GAP — SCRIPTED vs. NATURAL SPEECH
   Friends is scripted television. Actors deliver lines with dramatic
   timing, exaggerated expressions, and studio-quality audio/video.
   Real HR interviews have:
   - More subdued, professional emotional expression
   - Webcam/meeting room quality (lower resolution, worse lighting)
   - Background noise, echo, poor microphones
   - Longer pauses and filler words ("um," "uh")

   Our mitigation: We freeze pretrained encoders (BERT, R3D-18) that
   learned general features from massive diverse datasets, so the model
   doesn't overfit to MELD-specific patterns.

2. CULTURAL HOMOGENEITY
   Friends features predominantly white American actors expressing
   emotions through Western cultural norms. Emotional expression varies
   significantly across cultures — what looks like "anger" in American
   culture may be normal assertiveness in another.

   Our mitigation: We acknowledge this as a limitation and recommend
   fine-tuning on diverse, organization-specific data for production.

3. CLASS IMBALANCE
   47% neutral dominance can bias the model toward predicting neutral.

   Our mitigation: Inverse-frequency class weighting in CrossEntropyLoss
   (models.py:compute_class_weights) — rare classes like disgust and
   fear get higher loss weights. We also use label smoothing (0.05) to
   prevent overconfident predictions.

4. SINGLE LANGUAGE (ENGLISH)
   MELD only contains English dialogue. Our system cannot analyze
   interviews conducted in other languages without model changes.

   Our mitigation: For multilingual support, we would swap BERT for
   multilingual BERT (mBERT) and use Whisper "large" which supports
   99 languages. The audio and video encoders are language-agnostic.

5. ENTERTAINMENT vs. PROFESSIONAL CONTEXT
   Emotions in Friends are often comedic — surprise and joy are
   frequently exaggerated for laughs. In HR contexts, emotions are
   more nuanced and restrained.


================================================================================
ANTICIPATED FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: "If MELD is from a TV show, how can you claim your system works for
    real HR interviews?"
A: We don't claim production-level accuracy on HR interviews. MELD gives
   us a FOUNDATION — the model learns what anger sounds like, what joy
   looks like, how positive text differs from negative text. These are
   universal emotional signals. The pretrained encoders (BERT trained on
   BookCorpus+Wikipedia, R3D-18 trained on Kinetics-400 real human
   actions) provide generalization beyond MELD. For production, we would
   fine-tune on a small labeled set of real HR interviews using transfer
   learning — the MELD-trained model gives us a far better starting
   point than training from scratch.

Q: "Why not use CMU-MOSEI? It's bigger (23,000+ utterances)."
A: CMU-MOSEI contains YouTube monologues — one person talking to a
   camera. It's not conversational. Our system analyzes multi-speaker
   interviews with turn-taking, interruptions, and speaker diarization.
   MELD's multi-party conversational structure matches our use case.
   Additionally, MOSEI uses a dimensional annotation scheme
   (valence/arousal), while MELD uses categorical emotions that are
   more interpretable for HR professionals.

Q: "Why not collect your own HR interview dataset?"
A: Three reasons:
   1. ETHICS: Recording and labeling real HR interviews requires
      institutional ethics board approval, informed consent from
      employees/candidates, and organizational buy-in — a significant
      process for a thesis timeline
   2. SCALE: We would need thousands of labeled utterances across all
      7 emotion classes. Real HR interviews are predominantly neutral,
      making it very hard to collect enough anger/fear/disgust samples
   3. ANNOTATION COST: Labeling 13,000 utterances across 3 modalities
      requires trained annotators and significant time/budget
   Using MELD as a starting point and fine-tuning on a smaller HR
   dataset is the practical approach we recommend for future work.

Q: "What accuracy does your model achieve on MELD?"
A: Our training logs show final test loss of 2.64 (combined emotion +
   sentiment cross-entropy). The model was trained for 24 epochs with
   best model selection on validation loss. Training loss converged from
   3.09 to 2.60. We track weighted precision and accuracy for both
   emotion and sentiment classification via sklearn metrics.
   [NOTE: Pull your exact accuracy/precision numbers from your
   TensorBoard logs or SageMaker output to cite specific percentages.]

Q: "How does your model compare to state-of-the-art on MELD?"
A: State-of-the-art models on MELD achieve ~65-67% weighted F1 for
   emotion (e.g., M2FNet at CVPR 2022). These models use more complex
   architectures — cross-modal transformers, graph neural networks for
   dialogue context, and larger encoders. Our model uses simpler
   concatenation fusion with frozen pretrained encoders, making it
   faster and more deployment-friendly. The goal was not to beat SOTA
   on MELD but to build a practical, deployable system for HR use.

Q: "47% of MELD is neutral. Doesn't your model just predict neutral?"
A: Without mitigation, yes. That's why we compute inverse-frequency
   class weights (models.py line 146-189) and pass them to
   CrossEntropyLoss. Rare classes like disgust (~3%) and fear (~2%)
   receive proportionally higher loss weights, forcing the model to
   learn their patterns despite fewer examples. We also use label
   smoothing (0.05) to prevent the model from being overconfident
   on the majority class.
